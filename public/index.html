📊 How risk levels are evaluated What Critical, High & Medium mean — and how each rating is assigned

Every risk on this page carries one of three severity ratings. The rating signals how urgently a risk needs attention based on the combined weight of four factors: how large the regulatory penalty or prohibition exposure is, how many companies and products it touches, how close the relevant rule or deadline is, and how severe the business consequence would be if left unaddressed.

Critical

An existential or business-stopping exposure. Usually tied to a binding prohibition or the sharpest penalties under the EU AI Act — up to €35M or 7% of global annual turnover for prohibited-practice breaches (Art. 5), or €15M / 3% for transparency and governance failures (Art. 50) — with imminent enforcement and broad reach across products and companies.

High

A major compliance or competitive exposure that demands active mitigation. Typically a high-risk-system obligation under the AI Act, a structural market, IP, or data-protection risk, or a requirement with a defined compliance deadline and a material financial or reputational downside.

Medium

A contained or emerging exposure to monitor. Often a forthcoming obligation, a narrower product or segment impact, or a softer guidance / voluntary standard where the timeline or business effect is not yet acute.

How each rating is decided

  • Regulatory penalty exposure — magnitude of fines or prohibition risk under the EU AI Act, the UK regime, the DMA, and similar frameworks.
  • Breadth of impact — number and scale of affected companies, products, and user bases (see the coloured severity tags on each item).
  • Enforcement proximity — how close the applicable rule or deadline is: in force now, phased in, or still proposed.
  • Business impact — severity of the financial, operational, IP, or reputational consequence if the risk is left unaddressed.

Ratings are a curated cross-reference of the Knowledge Base, Competitor Web, EU AI Act, UK regulation, and proprietary research documents — not an automated score. Use the filters above to focus on a single level.
